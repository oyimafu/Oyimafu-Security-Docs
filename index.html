Task 0

Create Resource Group & Foundry Resource

Before you can secure anything, you need a place to put it. In Azure, a Resource Group is like a folder that holds all the related services for a project. You'll create one, then set up a Microsoft Foundry resource — the Azure service used to build and manage AI projects.

1
Open Microsoft Edge and navigate to https://portal.azure.com. This is the Azure Portal — your main dashboard for managing all Azure services.
2
In the lab instructions panel (outside the virtual machine), click the Info tab — it looks like a circled ⓘ icon. Click Cloud Express Pass (Azure) to reveal your temporary login credentials.
3
On the Azure sign-in page, enter the provided Username and click Next. On the next screen, enter the Temporary Access Pass and click Sign in.
4
In the top search bar of the Azure Portal, type Resource groups and select it from the results. Click + Create to start creating a new one.
5
In the Resource group name field, enter rg-foundry-lab. Then click Review + create, followed by Create.
6
Open a new browser tab and go to https://ai.azure.com — this is the Microsoft Foundry portal. Sign in with the same lab credentials. Close any welcome tips or quick-start panes that appear.
7
In the top toolbar, enable the New Foundry toggle if it isn't already on. When prompted, create a new project with any unique name. Expand Advanced options and fill in the following:

• Foundry resource: Use the default name
• Subscription: Your Azure subscription
• Resource group: Select rg-foundry-lab (the one you just created)
• Region: Choose any available region
💡 Click Create and wait for the project to be set up. If a Welcome window pops up, go ahead and close it.
🛡️
Task 1

Enable Protections for AI Workloads

Now that your resources exist, you need to turn on security monitoring for them. You'll enable two specific plans inside Microsoft Defender for Cloud:


Defender CSPM (Cloud Security Posture Management) continuously scans your cloud resources and gives you recommendations to fix security gaps — think of it as a security health check.


AI Services Plan adds real-time threat detection for AI interactions — it monitors what's happening with your AI workloads as they run and alerts you to anything suspicious.

1
Go to https://portal.azure.com. In the search bar at the top, type Microsoft Defender for Cloud and select it.
2
In the left sidebar, find and expand the Management section, then click Environment settings.
3
On the Environment settings page, scroll down and expand the Azure dropdown. Click on your subscription to open its settings.
4
On the Defender plans page, find Defender CSPM and toggle it to On. Then click Save.
📝 If a pop-up says "insufficient permissions", click Turn on the plan anyway, then click OK. This is normal in lab environments.
5
On the same page, scroll to the Cloud Workload Protection (CWPP) section. Find AI services and toggle it to On. Click Save.
6
Confirm that both Defender CSPM and AI services now show as On on the Defender plans page.
✅ You've successfully enabled posture management and real-time threat protection for your AI workloads in Microsoft Defender for Cloud.
📋
Task 2

Implement Security Policies for AI Services

Enabling Defender is reactive — it detects issues after the fact. Azure Policy is proactive: it enforces rules so certain misconfigurations can't happen in the first place. In this task, you'll assign a built-in policy that ensures your Azure AI Services resources are not exposed to the public internet without proper network controls.

1
In the Azure Portal, click Home at the top-left to return to the home page. Then click Subscriptions and select your Azure subscription.
2
In the left sidebar of your subscription, expand Settings and click Policies.
3
On the Policies page, expand Authoring in the sidebar and click Definitions. This shows you all available Azure policies.
4
In the search field, type Azure AI Services. From the results, click the policy named "Azure AI Services resources should restrict network access".
5
On the top-left of the policy page, click Assign policy. On the Basics tab, leave all defaults as-is. Click Review + create, then Create.
6
You should see a notification confirming that your policy assignment succeeded.
📝 Policy assignments take 5–15 minutes to take effect. Once active, any Azure AI Services resource that allows unrestricted network access will be automatically flagged as non-compliant.
✅ You've successfully assigned a security policy to enforce network restrictions on AI services.
🔐
Task 3

Control Access to AI Workloads Using Microsoft Entra ID

The final layer of protection is access control. Even if your AI resources are monitored and policy-compliant, you still need to control who can use them and with what level of permission.


You'll use Azure RBAC (Role-Based Access Control) to assign the Azure AI Developer role to a user account. This role gives just enough permission to build and test AI workloads — without granting full admin access.

1
From the Azure Portal home page, click Resource groups and select rg-foundry-lab — the resource group you created in Task 0.
2
Inside the resource group, find and click the resource with the type Foundry.
3
In the left sidebar of your Foundry resource, click Access control (IAM). This is where you manage who has access to this resource.
4
Click + Add, then choose Add role assignment from the dropdown.
5
On the Add role assignment page, stay on the Job function roles tab. In the search box, type Azure AI Developer and select that role. Click Next.
6
On the Members tab, select User, group, or service principal, then click + Select members. In the flyout panel that opens, search for and select your tenant account, then click Select.
7
Click Next to reach the Review + assign tab. Review your selection, then click Review + assign to confirm.
8
Back on the Access control (IAM) page, click the Role assignments tab. You should see a notification confirming the role assignment was successful, and your account should now appear in the list with the Azure AI Developer role.
✅ You've successfully assigned the Azure AI Developer role to your account on the Foundry resource using Microsoft Entra ID.
🎉

Exercise Complete!

You've secured an AI workload environment from the ground up — enabling threat detection, enforcing network policies, and controlling access. These are foundational practices for any cloud security professional working with AI services on Azure.
